# Personas

> Last updated: 2026-06-18

Who DumpIt is built for. The product targets a single primary persona
for V1. Pair with [`vision.md`](vision.md) for the problem framing.

## Pessoa ocupada (the overwhelmed professional)

The product's primary — and currently only — paying persona.

### Who

A Brazilian professional between 25 and 40 with a full plate: job,
freelance work, personal errands, family obligations. They may work
in tech, services, small business, or as a freelancer. The
discriminator is not the industry — it's the feeling: **"I have too
much to do and I don't know where to start."**

They are not a productivity enthusiast. They do not read GTD books or
watch YouTube videos about "my Notion setup." They want to get things
done, not optimize the system they use to get things done.

### Today's behavior

- Keeps a notes app (iPhone Notes, Google Keep) with an unstructured
  list of things to do, rarely reviewed.
- Uses WhatsApp to send voice messages to themselves as reminders.
- Starts using task apps every few months, abandons them within a week
  because setting up the system takes longer than doing the tasks.
- Relies on memory and anxiety as their primary task management system.

### Pain points

| Pain | Why it matters |
| ---- | -------------- |
| Setup cost of existing tools | Templates, categories, priority systems — all feel like homework before you can start working |
| English-first interfaces | Even if they understand English, the friction of thinking in Portuguese and writing in English adds cognitive load |
| Breaking down "I have a lot to do" into structured tasks | The hardest step is the first one: naming and scoping the work |
| Task managers that require discipline to maintain | A system that requires daily maintenance is another thing to forget to do |
| Free plans that expire or cripple the experience | "I can't trust this will be here next month" |

### Technical level

**Medium.** Comfortable with smartphones, WhatsApp, Instagram, and
mobile apps in general. Has used Google Docs, a calendar app, and at
least one task app (Todoist, Microsoft To Do, Notion, or similar).
Has not used Notion as a database or Jira — the "setup your workspace"
class of tools bounced them immediately.

Primary device is a **smartphone**. May also use a laptop for work.

### Motivation

- **Reduce the cognitive overhead** of holding everything in their head.
- **Start the day knowing what to do** instead of figuring it out as
  the day unfolds.
- **Feel less stressed** at the end of the day — not more productive
  in the abstract, but less overwhelmed in the concrete.
- **Find something that actually sticks.** They've tried other apps.
  They want one that works the way they think, not the way a
  productivity blogger thinks.

### Affordability

R$25/mês is within reach for a working professional, especially when
framed as the cost of a coffee. The value proposition must be clear
before asking for payment — the free trial (1 AI call vitalício) is
the proof-of-value moment. If the first AI parse impresses them,
conversion is plausible.

Price sensitivity is real but not extreme: the user is not comparing
to free alternatives (they've already failed with those). They're
comparing to the stress of not having a system.

## Casual user (named, not detailed)

Someone who creates an account, uses the free Kanban manually, and
never hits the AI gate. They represent the majority of signups by
count. DumpIt provides them genuine value (free, no ads, no expiry)
and may convert them eventually if their workload grows or if a
friend on the paid plan demonstrates the AI flow.

We do not design features specifically for this persona in V1 — the
free tier they get is the natural outcome of the paid-tier product.

## Personas explicitly out of scope

- **Teams and organizations.** When the buyer is a company paying for
  multiple seats, the product requirements (admin, permissions,
  reporting, SSO) are a different product.
- **Power users wanting a second brain.** Notion, Obsidian, and
  Roam serve this persona. DumpIt is not a personal knowledge
  management tool.
- **Children and students.** Not targeted; not excluded. But product
  decisions are not optimized for this group.
